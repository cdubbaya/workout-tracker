# Scoring is relative to the individual, and the Session Cap is the Session Goal

XP is earned per Counted Rep and valued by Variant, but a session's XP is capped at that user's own Session Goal — a number derived from their Max Test. Everyone tops out at their own calibrated number, so a beginner who hits their goal in knee reps earns exactly what an athlete hitting theirs in standard reps earns.

This exists to solve co-ed fairness, which is a requirement rather than a nicety: a mixed friend challenge must not systematically reward the participants who can do standard push-ups.

## Why no fixed weighting could have solved it

The obvious approach is to price the movements — knee at 5, standard at 10 — and let absolute totals compete. It cannot be made to work:

- **knee 5 / standard 10** — matching 50 standard reps takes 100 knee reps, and 100 knee reps is harder than 50 standard for most people. The weighting punishes the people it is meant to protect.
- **knee 10 / standard 10** — everyone does knee reps for the same XP at less cost. Race to the bottom, and volume climbs.
- **knee above standard** — the same race, inverted.

The reason there is no working constant: **the intensity of a movement is a property of the person–movement pair, not of the movement.** A knee push-up is near-maximal for one person and a warm-up for another. Anything that prices the movement instead of the effort will be unfair to someone. Relative-to-self scoring is the only version that closes.

The Max Test is therefore deliberately Variant-agnostic — as many as you can, in whatever form you can — so that the baseline it produces already contains the user's real capability without anyone having to declare a category.

## Consequences

- Grinding past the Session Goal earns nothing, which preserves the vision doc's fourth principle and its volume-injury argument without a separate mechanism. One cap does both jobs.
- Cumulative-XP friend challenges ("first to 10,000") become consistency races rather than volume races, and are fair across mixed groups for the same reason session scoring is.
- The Daily Cap — a separate backstop over free-form logging — must never sit below the prescribed Session Goal, or the program would prescribe work the cap forbids. It is the greater of 1,000 XP or the user's Session Goal, plus a fixed free-form allowance.
- Session Goal calibration becomes safety-critical. A miscalibrated goal is now a miscalibrated dose, not just a bad target.
